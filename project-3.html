Theyyam Sacred Ritual Kannur, Kerala

When Human Becomes God

Part II — The Fire and the Divine

Where does the line between extreme devotion and hope actually lie? I asked this question once before, at Kulashekarapattinam, Tamil Nadu. Now, in Kannur, Kerala, I found a different answer.

For this series, I photographed three forms of Theyyam. Kandanar Kelan Theyyam tells the story of a hunter who died in a forest fire and became a deity. During the ritual, the performer walks through fire, embodying the god himself. Wayanattukulavan Theyyam, which follows Kandanar Kelan in the ritual sequence, is connected to the story of his transformation into a deity. Kutti Chathan Theyyam, associated with the mischievous and playful son of a god, carries a completely different energy, reflected in the playful nature of its performer.

But across all three, something remained the same. The transformation was not merely in the elaborate makeup, costumes, rituals and movements. For those few hours, the performers were no longer treated simply as people performing a role. They were worshipped as gods. People approached them for blessings, bowed before them and placed their faith in them. And somewhere within that space, the line between the human and the divine seemed to disappear.

"Standing there with my camera, I found myself questioning that separation too. Was I photographing a man performing as a god, or a god being expressed through a man?"
